# Product Strategy: HelioDesk Positioning Reset

## Winning aspiration

Within 90 days, make HelioDesk the clearest choice for mid-market support teams that need automated resolutions to remain controllable and defensible.

## Where to play

**Target market**

- Mid-market SaaS companies with 100–1,000 employees.
- Primary operator: VP or Head of Customer Support.
- Buying-group expansion: Support Operations, COO, compliance, security, and risk stakeholders when they set approval criteria.
- Beachhead: Active opportunities where control, grounding, auditability, or escalation safety already appears in evaluation criteria.

**Not playing**

- A new “resolution governance control plane” category already associated with ResolveGrid.
- Buyers requiring complete enterprise certification coverage within the 90-day window.
- A broad “every support team” claim that ignores buying-group and proof differences.

## How to win

**Advantage hypothesis**

HelioDesk combines policy grounding, human approval, escalation controls, and deployment speed. ResolveGrid owns a governance-first narrative, while Intercom and Zendesk lead with resolution rate and suite breadth. HelioDesk can test whether control without implementation drag is a distinct reason to choose it.

**Proof boundary**

- Generally available: policy grounding, human approval, escalation, and human override.
- Beta: audit log and cost-per-resolution reporting.
- Directional only: 99.3% policy citation from an internal audited sample and 41% median escalation reduction among four selected beta customers.

## Capabilities required

- A shared claim ledger that prevents unqualified compliance, safety, and accuracy claims.
- Sales qualification that identifies ResolveGrid and cross-functional veto holders early.
- Buyer testing that compares preserve, refresh, and replacement frames with one script.
- Proof packaging that separates generally available capabilities from beta and directional evidence.

## 90-day roadmap

### Now: days 1–30

- Draft the controlled, defensible resolution hypothesis.
- Test it beside the current and refresh-only frames.
- Update discovery fields and the ResolveGrid battlecard.
- Build the claim ledger and installed-base continuity story.

### Next: days 31–60

- Use the strongest frame in a bounded set of ten live opportunities.
- Track category comprehension, buying-group coverage, proof objections, and progression.
- Decide whether audit logging or certification evidence is a named deal gate.

### Commit: days 61–90

- Replace the lead frame only if buyer comprehension and opportunity evidence improve.
- Keep deployment speed and deflection as subordinate proof.
- Revert to a refresh if the new frame confuses current customers or fails to change evaluation criteria.

## Management system

The VP Marketing owns frame testing, the CRO owns live-deal evidence, the CPO owns proof readiness, Legal and Security own claim approval, and the CEO owns the day-60 commitment.

## Decision

Do not preserve the current position. Run a reversible replacement test, then commit or narrow it at day 60. The evidence supports the direction of change, not a final category name.
