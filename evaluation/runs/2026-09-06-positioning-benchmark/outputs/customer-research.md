# HelioDesk Customer Research Synthesis

**Mode:** Analyze existing assets
**Evidence supplied:** Interview and loss-review excerpts, buyer-language frequency, category checks, reference-customer descriptions, commercial trends, competitive events, leadership responses, and product proof
**Decision use:** Determine whether the current positioning still matches the customer’s buying problem

## Sample and confidence limits

The pack contains five quoted customer or prospect excerpts, a twelve-term buyer-language corpus without raw counts, category responses from ten prospects, descriptions from five reference customers, four ResolveGrid losses, and five leadership responses. The sources are recent enough for the as-of date, but the selection method, interview guide, segment distribution, and complete transcripts are unavailable.

No persona or market-wide conclusion should be treated as validated from this sample. Themes below are ranked only within the supplied evidence.

## Top themes

### 1. Buyers need resolutions they can defend

**Frequency:** High within the supplied pack. Ten of the twelve frequent buyer terms concern grounding, control, quality, governance, auditability, or intervention. Three of ten prospects independently place HelioDesk in a governance-oriented category.

**Intensity:** High. The language points to organizational risk, not feature preference.

**Representative evidence:**

- “I don't need another copilot. I need resolutions I can defend.” — COO, lost prospect
- “We bought it to deflect tickets. Now I need proof the system didn't invent policy.” — Head of CX, customer
- “HelioDesk gets bots live fast, but our compliance team asks who approved each answer.” — VP Support, customer

**Implication:** The current deflection claim describes the old job. Current buyers want proof, approval, and controllability around automated resolution.

**Confidence:** Medium. The pattern appears across customers and losses, but the small quoted sample and missing raw corpus prevent a high-confidence segment claim.

### 2. HelioDesk owns useful controls but does not name them

**Frequency:** High. The homepage omits ten of the twelve frequent buyer terms. A customer explicitly says the escalation controls are stronger than the website suggests.

**Product match:** Policy grounding, human approval, escalation controls, and human override are generally available. Audit logging and cost-per-resolution reporting remain beta.

**Implication:** The positioning gap is not only a product gap. Market language and product proof have separated.

**Confidence:** High for the message-product mismatch inside this pack. Lower for any claim that a replacement position will win.

### 3. Buyers no longer repeat the intended category

**Frequency:** Nine of ten prospects use a category other than “AI support copilot.” Four of five reference customers also describe HelioDesk another way.

**Implication:** Preserve-and-polish is not supported. The company needs at least a tested category-frame replacement.

**Confidence:** Medium. The direction is clear, but the sample is too small to select a final category name.

### 4. The buying group is expanding

**Frequency:** Compliance, risk, COO, CISO, and operations concerns appear beside the existing Support buyer.

**Implication:** Qualification and proof must work for veto holders and operating stakeholders, not only the budget owner.

**Confidence:** Medium-low. The pack shows the signal but not buying-group prevalence across the full pipeline.

## Jobs to be done

| Job | Evidence status | Positioning consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Reduce support workload and cost | Established in current position and installed base | Keep as continuity value. |
| Deploy support automation without a long implementation | Repeated by leaders and references | Use as supporting proof, not the lead category. |
| Prove that automated answers follow approved policy | Repeated in current buyer language | Make grounding and traceability visible. |
| Retain human control over exceptions | Present in product and buyer terms | Show approval, escalation, and override mechanics. |
| Defend resolution quality to risk and operations stakeholders | Present in losses and market direction | Expand the audience and evaluation criteria. |

## Contradictions

- Leadership says speed, cost, controls, adoption, and implementation risk are different reasons customers choose HelioDesk.
- The installed base remembers deflection, while recent lost prospects ask for defensibility.
- Product proof is moving toward governance, but some of the strongest evidence is beta or internally validated.
- ResolveGrid owns explicit governance language, so copying the phrase would reduce differentiation.

## Research gaps that could change the decision

1. Raw buyer-language counts by role, segment, deal outcome, and date.
2. Ten interviews split among current customers, recent wins, and losses, with unprompted category and decision-criteria questions.
3. Win-loss coding for every Q1 and Q2 opportunity, including buying-group members and veto reasons.
4. Installed-customer comprehension of a new frame and evidence of churn or expansion risk.
5. Independent validation of the policy-citation method and the selected beta-customer escalation result.

## Research conclusion

The evidence rejects preserving the current position unchanged. It supports a replacement hypothesis centered on controlled, defensible resolution, with deployment speed and deflection retained as continuity proof. Customer research alone does not prove the final category or authorize full rollout. Run a same-script comparison of the current frame, a refresh, and the replacement hypothesis before the day-60 commitment.
