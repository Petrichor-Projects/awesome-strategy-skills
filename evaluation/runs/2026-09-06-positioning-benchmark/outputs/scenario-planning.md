# HelioDesk Positioning Reset: Scenario Plan

## Known conditions

- The current “AI copilot” frame is rarely repeated by prospects.
- Commercial performance deteriorated across win rate, sales cycle, discounting, and retention.
- Buyers now raise governance, proof, and control concerns.
- HelioDesk already has generally available grounding, approval, and escalation controls.
- A full rebuild or new enterprise certification is outside the 90-day boundary.

## Load-bearing uncertainties

1. **Buying criterion:** Will governance become a primary selection criterion, or remain a late-stage risk screen?
2. **Proof readiness:** Can HelioDesk turn current controls and beta evidence into credible proof without overclaiming?

## Four coherent scenarios

### 1. Control becomes the shortlist

Governance moves to the front of the buying process, and HelioDesk can substantiate the controls it already ships. Cross-functional buying groups grow. ResolveGrid’s language keeps setting the evaluation.

- **What breaks first:** The current homepage and qualification script.
- **Move now:** Test a controlled-resolution frame, identify risk and operations stakeholders, and package control proof.
- **Indicator:** At least 6 of the next 10 qualified opportunities name auditability, grounding, approval, or escalation among their top three criteria.
- **Owner and cadence:** CRO, reviewed weekly.

### 2. Proof gap

Governance becomes central, but buyers reject HelioDesk’s evidence because audit logging, cost reporting, and certification coverage remain incomplete.

- **What breaks first:** Broad trust language creates diligence friction.
- **Move now:** Distinguish generally available controls from beta evidence and publish claim boundaries internally.
- **Option:** Prioritize audit-log reliability and the missing certification evidence if loss reviews show those are selection gates.
- **Indicator:** Two qualified opportunities accept the positioning but block progression on validation, certification, or beta status.
- **Owner and cadence:** CPO with Security and Legal, reviewed weekly.

### 3. Governance stays a qualifier

Buyers still prefer the established “AI support automation” frame. Governance matters, but only as evidence that automation is safe enough to buy.

- **What breaks first:** A category-replacement effort confuses the market.
- **Move now:** Refresh the existing frame around defensible resolution quality without claiming a new category.
- **Indicator:** Fewer than 3 of 10 prospects use control language unprompted, but at least 5 request control proof during diligence.
- **Owner and cadence:** VP Marketing, reviewed every two weeks.

### 4. Continuity wins

Control demand is weaker than the recent losses suggest, and mature proof remains delayed. Installed customers and new prospects still buy faster deployment and unit economics.

- **What breaks first:** A hard repositioning erodes recognition without improving selection.
- **Move now:** Preserve deployment speed, add qualified control proof beneath it, and delay category replacement.
- **Indicator:** The current frame matches or beats the controlled-resolution hypothesis in message comprehension and qualified conversion across ten live opportunities.
- **Owner and cadence:** CRO and VP Marketing, reviewed at day 30 and day 60.

## Moves that make sense in every scenario

- Correct the battlecard and qualification process so ResolveGrid and risk stakeholders are identified early.
- Build a claim ledger separating generally available, beta, internal-sample, and unsupported statements.
- Test the current frame, a refresh, and a replacement hypothesis with the same buyers and decision criteria.
- Keep deployment speed and ticket deflection available as continuity proof.
- Set 30-day, 60-day, and 90-day decision reviews rather than waiting for a quarterly planning cycle.

## Contingent moves

- Replace the external lead frame only when buyer-language and live-opportunity evidence support it.
- Accelerate audit-log and certification work only when they become named deal gates.
- Expand the buying-group narrative to COO and CISO only where account evidence shows active ownership or veto power.

## Options worth buying now

- Create two proof packages: generally available controls and beta evidence.
- Add governance fields to qualification without changing compensation during the test.
- Prepare installed-base messaging that presents the new controls as an extension of deflection value.

## Monday decision

Run a reversible replacement test. Do not roll out a final category claim yet. The VP Marketing owns the message test, the CRO owns opportunity evidence, and the CPO owns proof readiness. Leadership commits or reverts at day 60 using the indicators above.
